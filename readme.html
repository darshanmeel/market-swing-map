Market swing map

One offline page per market: how far it is below its all-time high, its real turning points, and what every past fall this deep did next. Each weekday after the close a new guess, a year ahead, is saved and scored against what really happens.

It covers about 145 markets, grouped in the Market list at the top of any page:

  • Indices: Nasdaq Composite, Nasdaq-100, S&P 500, Dow Jones, Dow Transports, Russell 2000, NYSE Composite, DAX 40, FTSE 100, CAC 40, Euro Stoxx 50, Stoxx Europe 600, Nikkei 225, KOSPI, Hang Seng, Shanghai Composite, Sensex, Nifty 50, S&P/TSX and ASX 200.
  • Commodities and crypto: gold, silver, WTI crude oil and Bitcoin.
  • Companies: the Nasdaq-100 and the 30 Dow stocks, as listed in COMPANIES in market_highs.py.

Every tile, chart and table has an i button that explains it in plain words.

Not investment advice and not a forecast. The pages show what happened after similar falls in the past; the next fall can behave unlike any of them.

Quick start

pip install yfinance pandas plotly
python market_highs.py              # every market (10-20 minutes)
python market_highs.py ^IXIC ^GSPC  # only these (Yahoo symbols)

Then open highs_ixic.html (or any highs_*.html) in a browser. The pages work offline; plotly.min.js is written next to them on every run. Generated pages are not kept in git; each machine builds its own.

How it works

All the history maths runs in Python once per run; the page only redraws charts when a setting changes. All-time highs use the full history Yahoo has for each market, so a high is a real record, not just the top of the shown window.

Daily guesses

Each run saves one guess per market for that day's close in predictions/<market>.json. A rerun on the same day replaces that day's guess; older days are never changed.

How a guess is made

  1. Take every past fall from an all-time high that got at least as deep as today and later recovered.
  2. Keep the 8 that got to today's depth at the most similar speed (days from the high).
  3. From the day each one first reached today's depth, replay its next year as % moves from today's close.

How it is scored

The chart shows one guess day at a time (pick it from the list), zoomed to 1 week, 1 month, 6 months or 1 year, with the real closes printed on the black line. After every close, each saved guess gets the real close added. A guess is inside while the real close sits between its lowest and highest scenario for that day, below or above when it leaves them, the sign that this fall is unlike the past ones.

  • Bold line: the past fall the real path has followed most closely so far; the others are faded.
  • Dot colour on the real path: the past fall closest to that day's close.
  • Summary bullets under the chart: the move since the guess, the fall it follows, inside or outside the scenarios, and the closest fall each day.

On a market's first run, the last 20 trading days' guesses are made from the data each day had, so scoring starts at once.

Running it every day

On Windows a scheduled task runs the script Monday to Friday at 23:00 (after the US close at 22:00 Central European time) and appends the output to predictions/run.log:

$a = New-ScheduledTaskAction -Execute "cmd.exe" -WorkingDirectory "<repo folder>" `
     -Argument '/c "python -P -E market_highs.py >> predictions\run.log 2>&1"'
$t = New-ScheduledTaskTrigger -Weekly -DaysOfWeek Monday,Tuesday,Wednesday,Thursday,Friday -At 23:00
$s = New-ScheduledTaskSettingsSet -StartWhenAvailable -RunOnlyIfNetworkAvailable -ExecutionTimeLimit (New-TimeSpan -Hours 2)
Register-ScheduledTask -TaskName "Swing map daily" -Action $a -Trigger $t -Settings $s

The task runs while the user is logged on; if the computer is off at 23:00 it runs at the next start.

Limits

  • Small samples: most markets have 5 to 20 past falls this deep, each with its own cause.
  • Gold, silver and oil use Yahoo's front-month futures, which start in 2000; their "all-time high" is the highest since then. WTI's negative closes of April 2020 are dropped, since % moves and a log scale need positive prices.
  • A run during market hours uses the live price as the last close; the evening run replaces it.
